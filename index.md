I am a postdoctoral researcher in the [Developmental Computational Psychiatry Lab](https://devcompsy.org/) led by Tobias Hauser at the University of Tübingen, and a guest scientist at the [Max Planck Institute for Biological Cybernetics](https://www.kyb.tuebingen.mpg.de/en).


I completed my PhD in Computational neuroscience in the [Fouragnan lab](https://www.elsa-fouragnan.com/) at the University of Plymouth, and previously worked in the [Visual Neuroscience lab](https://www.opto.umontreal.ca/neurosciences/) at Montreal University (Canada), where I modeled the neural network of cortico-pulvino-cortical interactions.


I hold bachelor's degrees in Psychology and Biology, and a master's in <font color="#3acdd7"><strong>big data, computational neuroscience</strong></font> and <font color="#3acdd7">modeling</font>.


My research combines <font color="#3acdd7">computational modelling</font>, neuroimaging and behavioural data to understand the mechanisms of <font color="#3acdd7">impulsivity, decision-making and reward processing</font>, and how they go awry in psychiatric conditions such as addiction and psychosis. I am particularly interested in transdiagnostic and developmental approaches that link computational markers of behaviour to their neural and clinical correlates, and in building open, theory-driven modelling tools for the computational psychiatry community. I currently use <font color="#3acdd7">real-time fMRI</font> to study how spontaneous fluctuations in brain activity shape decision-making, and study the neural mechanisms of information gathering.
